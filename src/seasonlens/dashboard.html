Market snapshot

Changes compare the last observation with the preceding observation, the last observation on or before the cutoff minus one calendar month, and the last observation on or before prior December 31. SMA200 distance uses 200 observations. Percentiles include the current observation and give ties half weight: 100 × (lower observations + half of equal observations) / count. They describe historical levels, not valuation or trading signals; dates and coverage are shown.

What changed the price in PLN?

Exact-date EUR commodity × PLN per EUR only. Total change = commodity change + FX change + interaction (the product of both changes). Prices are matched on dates without carrying quotes forward. This is a benchmark conversion, not an executable quote.

Compare markets from a common start of 100

Each selected positive series starts at 100 on the first shared observation date in the chosen range. Only dates shared by all selected instruments are plotted; no interpolation. This is a price index, not an investable total return.

Risk and momentum

Historical volatility

Two lines show unannualized sample standard deviation of daily log price changes over 20 and 60 observed intervals, expressed as a percentage. Positive prices are required throughout each volatility window. Missing calendar days are not inserted; futures roll effects can affect measured volatility.

RSI 14 — separate momentum scale

Wilder smoothing starts with 14 price changes. RSI has its own fixed 0–100 axis; dashed 30 and 70 levels are visual references. Flat windows show 50, gain-only windows 100 and loss-only windows 0. RSI is descriptive and is not a buy/sell instruction.

Relationships between markets

Correlations of percentage changes

Pearson correlations compare simple percentage changes over exactly matching start and end dates. Constant or insufficient windows show —. The matrix uses the last selected number of matched return pairs for each pair, so dates and counts can differ. USD/PLN = EUR/PLN ÷ EUR/USD: these currency series are mathematically dependent, not independent evidence. Correlation does not imply causation.

Wheat minus corn

Wheat price minus corn price on identical observation dates. Blue: difference; black dashed: mean over the displayed range. This compares continuation benchmarks; delivery months, specifications and roll rules may differ.

Seasonal distribution and price scenarios

Seasonal median and middle 50% range

Each completed historical year contributes one available monthly average with equal weight. Purple: median; pale purple: 25th–75th percentile (linear interpolation); blue: mean. Commodity and FX scenario

Technical analysis — last 12 months

Six lines: daily price, SMA20/100/200 and Bollinger upper/lower = SMA20 ± 2 population standard deviations of the last 20 observations. The middle Bollinger line is already SMA20. Indicators use daily observations, without inserting weekends or missing sessions. Earlier history warms up indicators before the displayed 12 calendar months; a full window is required. FX panels use reference observations. The Y-axis fits visible lines; CSV exports all cutoff-visible observations and indicators.

Analyze a CSV privately in this browser

Your selected file is processed in this tab. It is not uploaded, sent to a provider, or saved by SeasonLens. Imported observations are cleared when you reload. Use Export to keep observations.

UTF-8 CSV, ISO dates YYYY-MM-DD, one series per file, maximum 12 MiB and 20,000 data rows. No thousands separators. Invalid dates, duplicate dates (including excluded future rows), nonfinite prices and malformed CSV reject the whole import. Values must be zero or have magnitude between 1e-100 and 1e100. Negative/zero levels remain usable where meaningful; percentage analyses require positive levels. Imported EUR/t data is not automatically joined to sample FX: that would mix your data with invented quotes.
