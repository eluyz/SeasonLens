Historical risk and stress episodes

For a buyer, a price increase is adverse; for a seller, a decrease is adverse. Signed estimates can be negative. Horizons count consecutive observed intervals, which may span more calendar days.

What do VaR, Expected Shortfall and stress episodes mean?

Historical VaR is an empirical upper quantile of adverse percentage changes. Expected Shortfall averages the worst tail with a fractional boundary weight. At least five equivalent tail observations are required: 100 changes at 95%, or 500 at 99%. Overlapping multi-observation changes are dependent. These estimates describe this history; they do not guarantee future outcomes. The exposure calculation applies a signed historical change to the current benchmark budget. It is not a futures investment profit or loss. Stress endpoints describe benchmark price paths and may include futures contract changes.

Completed-year return seasonality and stability

How do I read stability and the uncertainty interval?

Monthly change compares the last observed price in a month with the last observed price in the immediately preceding calendar month. Missing months break this comparison. Each completed year has equal weight, and the current calendar year is excluded. Early and late means use a fixed calendar split; the leave-one-year-out range shows the means obtained after removing each contributing year. The approximate 95% interval describes the historical mean under a seeded moving two-year block bootstrap. It preserves two-year blocks and jointly resamples all months, but cannot remove small-sample or changing-regime limitations. At least eight contributing years and 90% valid bootstrap replicates are required. It is not a prediction interval, p-value or significance verdict.

Commodity and FX contributions to PLN price risk

Uses the selected commodity's original EUR/t quotes and its explicitly compatible original EUR/PLN series. Exact start and end dates of observed changes must match; unmatched intervals are omitted. The selected PLN/t display does not replace these source quotes.

Why can a contribution be negative or exceed 100%?

The variance of the sum of commodity and FX log changes includes their covariance. Each Euler contribution receives its own variance plus that covariance. Opposing movements can produce a negative contribution or a share above 100%; these values are retained. Zero total variance has no defined percentage shares. This is a historical variance decomposition, not proof of causation or the effect of a hedge.

Walk-forward forecast comparison

Compare last price, expanding-history drift and a trailing 20-observation mean. Each historical forecast uses only observations available at its origin and is scored against a later observed quote. Evaluation uses the chosen period; earlier supplied history warms up the models.

How do I interpret forecast errors?

MAE is mean absolute error; RMSE is root mean squared error. Both use the selected price or FX units; smaller errors are better on this sample. Skill compares an error with the last-price baseline on identical eligible origins: positive means a smaller error, negative means a larger error. Undefined baseline errors remain unavailable. The fixed evaluation uses at least 200 training observations and at most 252 recent eligible origins per horizon. Horizons count observed intervals, not guaranteed trading days. This comparison does not show investment profitability and does not select or tune a model using future prices.

What is stocks-to-use?

Stocks-to-use is ending stocks divided by total use, multiplied by 100. It describes the balance for the explicitly named commodity, geography and marketing year. Releases and revisions remain dated. Only releases on or before the selected analysis cutoff are shown. A private normalized CSV is labelled USER_FILE; invented examples are SYNTHETIC. Neither is presented as an official USDA dataset or used automatically in a price forecast. A historical test would need the exact estimate vintage available at its forecast origin.
